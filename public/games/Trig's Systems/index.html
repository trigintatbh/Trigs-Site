how it works
The Trigian calendar defines its day as exactly 100,000 vels (TSM base time unit).
[1 vel ≈ 0.864 SI seconds — so 100,000 vels ≈ 86,400 s = 1 solar day]
Time is fully decimal: 10 ors × 100 mins × 100 vels — every unit divides cleanly by 10.
1 or = 10,000 vels · 1 min = 100 vels · 1 vel = base tick.
Structure: 10 months × 36 days = 360 days, followed by 5 Stilldays = 365 days/yr.
The Stilldays are a quiet period outside the month structure between the end of one year and the start of the next.
Leap rule: a 6th Stillday added every 4 years, skipped every 128 years. drift: ~0.00031 d/yr — beats the Gregorian calendar (~0.0075 d/yr)
Day zero: 0000-01-01 local proleptic Gregorian.

temperature scale (zennic)
Freeze and boil are defined values in TSM — not approximations. The SI conversions below are the approximations. Scale: 0 zn = water freezes, 10,000 zn = water boils → 1 zn = 0.01 K exactly. Absolute zero = −27,315 zn (exact defined constant).
